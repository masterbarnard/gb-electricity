GB Electricity — live generation, demand and carbon intensity for Great Britain

Every few minutes, Great Britain’s electricity system rearranges itself — wind rises, gas throttles back, a cable to Norway reverses direction. This dashboard shows that happening, from the public data, as it happens.

It puts the whole system on one page: what is generating and from what, how much the country is actually using, which way each interconnector is flowing, how clean this half hour is, and what it costs to be out of balance. Nothing is modelled that could be measured, and everything that is modelled says so on the page.

What you can see

  • Generation by fuel type — gas, nuclear, wind, biomass, hydro, pumped storage and the rest, ranked and shown as shares, updated every five minutes.
  • Two demand figures, not one — what the transmission system can see, and what the country actually used once rooftop solar is added back in.
  • Interconnectors link by link — France, Norway, Denmark, the Netherlands, Belgium and Ireland, each with its direction and its share of capacity.
  • Carbon intensity and system frequency — how clean the mix is this half hour, and the real-time balance of the whole system.
  • Every window downloadable — any panel expands to fill the screen, and any data window exports as a timestamped CSV.

There is no account, no tracking and no server: it is a single HTML page that calls public APIs from your browser and draws what comes back.

Sources: Elexon Insights (BMRS), the NESO Carbon Intensity API and Sheffield Solar PV_Live. How each figure is derived, what these feeds cannot see, and the places this data is most often misread are set out in About this dashboard, in the footer.
